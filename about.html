The cap stack.

Who does what behind your plan.

Publicly-listed parent
CUEN
OTCQB: CUEN

The public company that holds the MVNO.

An MVNO (Mobile Virtual Network Operator) is the regulated company that signs wholesale agreements with carrier networks and stands behind every line on every bill. For Bitcoin Mobile, that company is CUEN.

CUEN trades on OTCQB under the ticker CUEN. That means real-world disclosure obligations, audited financials, and accountability that a privately-held startup doesn’t have. The carrier behind your phone bill files quarterly reports.

The network
World Mobile

The infrastructure under the hood.

World Mobile is a decentralized telecom network. Coverage is provided by independently-operated AirNodes instead of a single legacy carrier. EarthNodes are the brain of the system, running decentralized identity (DID), proof-of-stake transaction validation, and telecommunications services on top of the World Mobile Chain, the network’s native ledger.

Two things this gives you that a normal carrier can’t: self-sovereign identity and verifiable infrastructure (every routing decision and authentication is auditable, end to end).

Bitcoin Mobile inherits that privacy and security, along with the principles behind them.

Why this stack.

Accountability from a public company, principles from a decentralized network.

A public company gives you accountability. SEC filings, audited financials, and a regulatory track that a privately-held startup can quietly disappear from.

A decentralized network gives you the principles. World Mobile’s architecture is built so your phone bill respects the same things Bitcoin does.

Most carriers are a single company with a big marketing budget. Bitcoin Mobile has three separate layers, and each one is accountable for its part.
