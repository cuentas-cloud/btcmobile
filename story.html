01 · The break2008

When the system broke.

In September 2008, Lehman Brothers collapsed. The financial crisis that followed was the worst since 1929. The response was unprecedented: trillions of dollars were created out of thin air to rescue the institutions that had caused the crisis.

Ordinary people lost homes, jobs, and savings. The institutions that lost everyone else’s money were rescued and then handed bonuses. Trust in the financial system collapsed. People started asking the obvious question: does money have to work this way?

02 · The proposalOctober 31, 2008

A nine-page paper changed money.

Six weeks after Lehman collapsed, an anonymous developer using the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto emailed a cryptography mailing list. Attached was a paper proposing electronic cash that could move directly between people, without a bank in the middle.

It solved a problem cryptographers had been chewing on for decades: how do you stop the same digital dollar from being spent twice without trusting a central authority? Satoshi’s answer used proof-of-work and a public ledger that anyone could verify.

03 · The first blockJanuary 3, 2009

The first block carried a message.

Satoshi mined the first block of the Bitcoin network, known as the genesis block, and embedded a line of text inside it. It was a headline from The Times of London, printed that same day.

The headline proved the block could not have been created before that date. It also made clear what Bitcoin was responding to.

04 · Principles

Four things Bitcoin actually stands for.

Leave the price and the headlines aside. At the protocol level, Bitcoin comes down to four ideas. Tap each one to read more.

01Fixed supply.

Only 21 million bitcoin will ever exist. The schedule is enforced by code that’s been running, untouched, since 2009. No central bank can vote to print more. The supply curve is the most predictable in monetary history.

02Your keys, your coins.

Bitcoin is the first money in history that you can fully self-custody at scale. If you hold the private keys, no bank, government, or company can freeze, seize, or roll back your money. The trade-off is that keeping them safe is your responsibility.

03Permissionless.

There’s no application form. No nationality requirement. No minimum balance. A teenager in Lagos and a hedge fund in New York interact with the exact same protocol on the exact same terms. Anyone can take part.

04Censorship-resistant.

A valid Bitcoin transaction will be processed by the network whether or not any particular intermediary approves of it. There’s no payment processor that can decline you, no acquiring bank that can revoke your access. The network is the rail.

05 · Sixteen years later

From a mailing list to a global asset.

Bitcoin started as a curiosity discussed by cryptographers. It is now held by individuals, public companies, and sovereign states. A short timeline of how it got here.

2009
Network launches.
Genesis block is mined. Bitcoin is a curiosity worth fractions of a cent.
2010

A programmer in Florida pays 10,000 BTC for two pizzas. The price of "real-world" Bitcoin is born.
2013

Cypriot depositors are forced to fund a bank rescue from their savings. Bitcoin’s price spikes as people search for an exit.
2016

2021

Held by individuals, public companies, and sovereign treasuries. Traded 24/7 across every time zone.
06 · Mobile, broken

Your phone bill has the same problem.

Mobile is the most invasive consumer product you own. Your carrier knows where you sleep, who you call, what you stream, and where your kid is at three on a Tuesday. They keep it for years, and most of it gets sold.

Until the FTC stepped in last year, a single broker called Mobilewalla had paired 500 million phone IDs with precise location histories and sold them to whoever was buying. ICE bought a similar tool from a vendor called Penlink to track specific people in real time. This sub-industry has existed for as long as smartphones have.

The systems underneath were built for a different era, when every party on the network was assumed trustworthy and nobody asked for privacy. Fifty years and several generations of wireless later, those systems have barely changed. Only the marketing has.

Bitcoin solved a problem like this in 2008 by building money on a network that doesn’t require trust. Bitcoin Mobile applies the same instinct to your phone bill.
